Before making coffee, rinse the paper filter with hot water and warm the cup so the drink stays at a comfortable temperature. Place fresh medium-ground coffee in the brewer, put it on a kitchen scale, and check that the scale reads zero before pouring. Pour a small amount of water in a slow circle to wet all the grounds, then wait thirty seconds while the coffee releases gas. Continue adding water gently from the centre toward the edge, keeping the stream away from the bare filter paper. If the water drains too quickly, choose a slightly finer grind for the next cup rather than pushing the coffee down. If the brew tastes harsh or bitter, try a coarser setting and allow the kettle to cool for a short time before pouring. A light roast often needs hotter water than a dark roast because its flavours are harder to extract from the beans. Keep a simple notebook of the coffee weight, water weight, temperature, grind setting, and total brewing time. Change only one setting between attempts so you can understand whether the adjustment actually improved the result. Clean the brewer thoroughly after use and store the remaining beans in a closed container away from light and heat. Coffee from different regions can taste fruity, nutty, floral, or chocolate-like, even when prepared with the same method. Compare each cup after it cools a little because very high temperatures can hide both sweetness and unpleasant flavours. After choosing a recipe, serve the coffee promptly and ask each person whether they prefer milk or sugar in their cup. Avoid leaving the finished drink on a hot plate because prolonged heating can damage the delicate aroma and flavour. When several friends visit, prepare separate small batches instead of filling the filter beyond the recommended capacity. Finally, return the equipment to its usual shelf and wipe the kitchen counter so the next brewing session starts cleanly.
